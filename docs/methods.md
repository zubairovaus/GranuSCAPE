# Computational method

Native CZI fluorescence planes are imported without intensity rescaling. Channel identities and XY calibration are validated, and original acquisition XML is retained. Cellpose is executed in an isolated environment with DAPI/zero/zero input for nuclei and AF488/DAPI/zero input for soma proposals. Inference uses percentile normalization at 1 and 99, a flow threshold of 0.4 and a cell-probability threshold of 0. Pixel classifiers trained in ilastik produce two classes for G3BP and three classes for eIF3η. The installed ilastik two-level threshold operator is applied using the corresponding unchanged threshold project. Probability arrays and all threshold outputs are retained.

The anatomical interface is inferred from a Gaussian-smoothed map (default sigma 12 µm) of nuclear centroids. An open contour adjacent to a large sparse region and a dense nuclear component is selected. A constrained cubic graph is fitted in interface-aligned coordinates, with diffuse extranuclear AF488 soma contrast providing bounded secondary support. The fit is extended to image edges and checked for self-intersection and excessive curvature. This model assumes an extended, smooth layer interface; frames lacking an appropriate interface require anatomical review.

Layer widths are estimated from local nuclear-density profiles on the two sides of the interface. Profiles are sampled at 4 µm intervals, with neighboring-profile median filtering and a first sustained drop below 60% of the local peak. Smooth side-specific widths are constrained between 5 and 60 µm. Profiles with insufficient evidence use an explicit 15 µm fallback; this behavior must be considered during visual review. Bright, sufficiently large somata near the density band can expand its sparse-side envelope. Punctum masks are not used to fit the anatomical interface.

Optional expert merges are validated for positive labels, connectivity and frame exclusion. Every extant soma seed is evaluated for contour refinement, without a minimum soma area, a preliminary contrast cutoff or an MCL-membership gate. Frame-truncated cells remain excluded. A local background sample and nonempty extranuclear seed signal are required; insufficient evidence is reported explicitly. Small signal samples are recorded in diagnostics. Candidate expansion uses a smoothed diffuse-signal threshold equal to background plus 40% of the soma-background difference, with a 10 µm reach limit. Original labels and neighboring seeds are protected. Overlap conflicts are resolved by distance to original masks with deterministic label-ID ties. Sensitivity is assessed at a 55% threshold; large or unstable proposals are rejected. A final measurement pass is necessary because contour changes affect area, nucleus association, local backgrounds and the MCL support envelope.

Nuclei are associated by a geometric score combining overlap coverage, relative depth, centrality and relative size. All overlap rankings are retained. The accepted primary requires sufficient nuclear coverage and size, score at least 0.55 and a margin of at least 0.15. Nuclear overlap is not interpreted as multinucleation. Morphotype candidates are assigned by a robustly scaled Gaussian mixture of log soma area, log primary nuclear area, log-transformed diffuse AF488 contrast and log nuclear DAPI intensity. Two-component support requires at least 30 complete profiles, BIC improvement at least 10, at least 10 profiles in each component and the expected size/contrast direction. High posterior membership and a soma-size midpoint criterion define mitral-like candidates. Missing-nucleus profiles may use a marginal model only when the full model is supported.

Punctum masks are analyzed separately for each marker. Pixel intersection and union are computed without double counting. Object counts use unique majority soma ownership; pixel coverage uses the complete positive mask clipped to the projected soma. The nucleus is not subtracted from the denominator. Areas projected onto any nuclear mask are reported separately. Perinuclear coverage is computed in the extranuclear 0–2 µm zone outside the accepted primary nucleus; distal coverage uses the remaining extranuclear soma. Eight angular sectors quantify variation of area-normalized coverage. The resultant length of object centroid angles describes angular concentration; it is undefined for cells without suitable objects. Raw nearest-neighbor distances require at least two assigned objects and are descriptive until evaluated against an appropriate geometric null model.

Regional MCL summaries include puncta without soma assignment. The CSV exports retain flagged and outside-region profiles so downstream analysis can apply an explicit review policy. Group-level hypothesis testing is outside this core package.


For visual quality review only, each complete channel is stretched using its 1st and 99.7th intensity percentiles, followed by independent CLAHE (128×128 native-pixel kernel, clip limit 0.005, 256 bins) and gamma 1.3. The RGB composite maps Rhoda/G3BP to red, AF488/eIF3η to green and DAPI to blue, then is dimmed to 38% behind contours. These display transformations do not enter segmentation, cell classification, intensity measurements or spatial statistics. Four inspection views and a separate numbered soma overlay are exported. Native-grid contours are emphasized for readability; areas must be read from masks/tables rather than measured from display PNGs.


Computational performance is measured by repeated complete processing of a prespecified CZI panel with fixed configuration and seed-controlled input order. A separately recorded first-image warm-up is followed by three measured repetitions, each writing fresh results. Monotonic wall-clock timers record top-level stages and nested inference/analysis substeps. Image-level median times are computed across repeats, and their distribution across distinct images is summarized by median and interquartile range. Warm-up, failures, workload sizes and hardware/software metadata are retained. See benchmark.md for exact inclusions and exclusions.
